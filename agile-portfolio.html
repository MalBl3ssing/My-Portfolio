Detailed technical reflection

Data investigation and Oracle SQL

I investigated Oracle schemas, reporting views and relevant fields to understand how accruals, rewards, partner information and categories were represented. DEV and PROD differences and access limitations meant that source assumptions sometimes needed confirmation.

Power BI modelling

I built and refined relationships and supporting dimensions. Incorrect cardinality or filter propagation could make apparently reasonable measures produce misleading outputs. I learned to inspect model behaviour before changing calculations.

Collaborative UI and frontend development

Between 13 and 20 October, I temporarily supported a colleague’s web application. I improved the existing Figma wireframes to align with an established company interface and implemented frontend work with React and Vite. This assignment required me to adapt from data reporting to visual interface design and frontend development. The manager then redirected me to Power BI reporting, so I handed the work back while the application was still being developed. This reinforced the importance of clear task ownership, collaboration and continuity when priorities change.

DAX calculations

My work included measures for points, participants, transaction values, contributions, trends and period comparisons. I learned to test intermediate results and filter context rather than repeatedly rewriting a measure without diagnosing the cause.

Categorisation and legacy logic

The Analysis report required investigating earn types, trend categories, accrual categories, batch types, descriptions and mapping sheets. Some categories depended on combinations of fields, showing why legacy Excel rules could not always be reproduced from one source column.

Validation and troubleshooting

I compared Power BI outputs with Oracle data and existing Excel reports, investigated mismatches and consulted colleagues when business rules were unclear. A mismatch did not necessarily mean Power BI was wrong; the difference could reflect manual Excel adjustments or undocumented logic.

Professional growth

My placement strengthened my technical knowledge and my ability to investigate ambiguous reporting problems, document findings and communicate with stakeholders. The October web-development assignment also strengthened my adaptability: I shifted from BI reporting to interface design and React implementation, collaborated with a colleague and handed work over when priorities changed. I learned that a useful contribution does not always mean completing an entire product; it can mean delivering a defined part of the work clearly enough for another developer to continue.
